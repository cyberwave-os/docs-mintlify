**Needs:** ~1 hour to a moving model, ~2 days to a live twin, 1–2 weeks to a trained policy · advanced · your robot, an edge computer (64-bit Raspberry Pi, Jetson, the robot's onboard computer, or a Mac for local development), Docker, Python 3.10+, a URDF with meshes · free to start; MuJoCo simulation uses [credits](/billing/credits)

This guide is for hardware makers and teams with custom hardware: a custom robot, an in-house prototype, or a niche industrial machine. It takes you in order from "is my robot already supported?" to "my twin moves my robot".

When you finish, anyone with access to your asset can spawn a twin of your robot, simulate it, drive the real machine from the browser or the SDK, record data, and put a trained policy in control. That holds whether the hardware is on the same desk or on the other side of the world.

File layouts, upload fields, limits, physics settings and driver config are in the [Asset and driver spec](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset).

## Check if your robot is already supported

Cyberwave ships **80+ devices** in its [catalog](https://cyberwave.com/catalog). Catalog devices like `the-robot-studio/so101` are assets that already exist, and many already have a driver. Check [Supported hardware](/overview/supported-hardware) first.

If your robot isn't there, you can integrate it yourself. Cyberwave connects **any** device with an API, serial interface or network protocol.

<Info>
  Teams typically bring a brand-new piece of hardware fully online with
  Cyberwave in about **2 days**, versus the **months** a bespoke integration
  usually takes, because the digital twin, transport, UI, workflows and
  data tooling already exist. You only supply the asset and the driver.
</Info>

## How it works

You build two things:

- **An asset.** The reusable definition of your hardware: its 3D meshes and kinematic model (a URDF). Every twin starts from an asset.
- **A driver.** A small **Docker container** that bridges your device's native interface to Cyberwave's MQTT layer. It publishes telemetry (joint states, odometry, camera frames, sensor data) up to the twin. It turns dashboard, SDK and workflow commands into actions on the device.

A **twin** is a live instance of the asset inside an [environment](/feature-reference/architecture/key-concepts). Edge Core runs your driver next to the hardware and links it to the twin.

Because integration happens at the driver layer, Cyberwave works with ROS 1 / ROS 2, VDA5050, OPC UA, Modbus, gRPC, REST and serial / USB. See [Supported protocols](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset#supported-protocols).

## Support levels

Pick a target level before you start. Each level builds on the one before it and has a test you can run yourself.

| Level | What works | Pass test |
|---|---|---|
| **L0 Model** | Kinematics and meshes render; the twin moves in the free browser Playground | `arm.joints.set(...)` in `cw.affect("playground")` moves the right link, in the right direction, within limits |
| **L1 Sim** | Physics is complete enough for MuJoCo (inertials, collisions, actuators) | A MuJoCo simulation starts with your twin in the scene, and the twin holds its pose without exploding or drifting |
| **L2 Telemetry** | The live twin mirrors the real robot | Move the robot by hand (or with its vendor tool). The twin follows, and `arm.joints.get()` in `cw.affect("live")` returns the real values |
| **L3 Control** | Declared commands, teleop, safe failure | Teleop from the browser moves the robot. The teleop overlay lists only commands the driver handles. Cutting the network mid-teleop leaves the robot in a safe state, and an alert appears on the twin |
| **L4 Learn** | Record, train, deploy | Record episodes, export them as a dataset, fine-tune a policy, assign it as the twin's controller, and watch it run on the robot |

**Time budget.** These are planning ranges for a team that already has a clean URDF and a working ROS 2 driver: L0 in about an hour, L2 in 1–2 days, L3 in 3–5 days, L4 in 1–2 weeks. Without a ROS 2 driver, add the time it takes you to write one against your vendor SDK.

## Prepare your URDF package

Cyberwave accepts a single **ZIP archive** with one main `.urdf` (or `.xacro`) file and every mesh, texture and material it references. Use relative paths that match the ZIP layout. Include inertials and joint limits now; L1 needs them.

```text
my-robot/
  urdf/
    robot.urdf
  meshes/
    base_link.stl
    arm_visual.obj
  textures/
    arm_albedo.png
```

The full packaging rules (file types, symlinks, size limit) are in [Asset package](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset#asset-package).

<Tip>
  Don't have a URDF yet? Most robots ship one with their ROS package, or you can
  export one from your CAD tool. For a sensor-only device (like a camera) you can
  start from a simple placeholder mesh and refine it later.
</Tip>

### Start from the sample URDF

Want a concrete reference? Download this sample drone URDF (a DJI Mini 4 Pro). It defines a `base_link` plus four propeller links connected by `continuous` joints, and references its meshes by path.

<Card title="Sample URDF: dji-mini-4-pro.zip" icon="file-zipper" href="/assets/dji-mini-4-pro.zip">
  A complete single-file URDF (5 links, 4 continuous joints, materials, mesh references). Unzip to read it, add your `meshes/` folder, and re-zip to upload.
</Card>

```xml
<robot name="dji_mini_4_pro">
  <link name="base_link">
    <visual>
      <geometry>
        <mesh filename="meshes/body.stl" scale="0.01 0.01 0.01"/>
      </geometry>
      <material name="drone_grey"/>
    </visual>
    <!-- collision + inertial omitted for brevity -->
  </link>

  <joint name="prop_front_left_joint" type="continuous">
    <parent link="base_link"/>
    <child link="prop_front_left"/>
    <origin xyz="-0.10764 0.06722 -0.00450" rpy="0 0 0"/>
    <axis xyz="-0.10884 0.23674 0.96546"/>
  </joint>
  <!-- prop_front_right, prop_back_left, prop_back_right joints follow the same pattern -->
</robot>
```

<Info>
  The downloadable sample references its meshes with `package://` URIs (a ROS
  convention). When you build your upload ZIP, switch those to **relative paths**
  that match the archive (for example `meshes/body.stl`) and include the
  referenced `.stl` files in a `meshes/` folder.
</Info>

### Validate it locally

The same parser library the platform uses is on PyPI. It has a Python API, not a CLI:

```bash
pip install cyberwave-robot-format
```

```python
from cyberwave_robot_format.urdf import URDFParser

schema = URDFParser().parse("my-arm/urdf/robot.urdf")
print(schema.validate())                       # validation issues
ctx = schema.extensions.get("parse_context", {})
print(ctx.get("warnings"), ctx.get("errors"))  # recovered quirks
```

The parser tolerates common URDF quirks (a missing `type` on a joint, say) and records each one in `parse_context`. Anything under `errors` means something in your file was discarded. Starting from MuJoCo? `cyberwave_robot_format.mjcf.MJCFParser` parses MJCF the same way.

**Done when:** `validate()` returns no issues and `parse_context["errors"]` is empty.

## Spawn a twin and test the model

An asset is a reusable definition. A twin is a live instance of it inside an environment. You can spawn one from the SDK or the dashboard.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="SDK">
    ```python
    from cyberwave import Cyberwave

    cw = Cyberwave()                         # reads CYBERWAVE_API_KEY
    arm = cw.twin("acme/catalog/my-arm")     # prints the environment URL
    cw.affect("playground")                  # free browser simulation
    print(arm.joints.list())
    arm.joints.set("joint_1", 30, degrees=True)
    ```
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    1. In the dashboard, create a **New Environment** and give it a name like `Acme Arm Lab`.
    2. Click **Add from Catalog** in the left panel and search for your asset. It appears in your workspace catalog.
    3. Add it and position it to roughly match where the real hardware sits.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

The twin isn't connected to anything yet. Note its **UUID** (in its properties panel). Your driver receives it at runtime.

**Done when:** every joint moves the right link in the right direction and stops at its limits. **You are at L0.**

## Declare capabilities and sensors

Capabilities decide which UI panels and SDK classes your twin gets. They live in the asset's universal schema under `extensions.cyberwave.capabilities`, next to a `sensors` list.

- `can_actuate` enables joint control and the re-calibrate action.
- `can_locomote` enables Missions.
- `can_grip` and `has_joints` enable the controller UI.
- A non-empty `sensors` list enables sensor windows.

The full table is in the [feature-to-capability matrix](/feature-reference/digital-twins).

Fix them on the asset page's onboarding checklist, in the JSON editor ([Editing a universal schema](/feature-reference/edit-universal-schema)), or from the SDK:

```python
asset = cw.assets.get("acme/catalog/my-arm")
cw.assets.patch_universal_schema(
    asset.uuid,
    path="/extensions/cyberwave/capabilities/can_grip",
    value=True,
    op="add",
)
```

For each sensor, set its `id`, `type` (`rgb`, `depth`, `lidar_3d`, `imu`, …), mount offset, and, for cameras, `width`, `height` and FOV. **The declared resolution must match the frames your driver actually publishes.** If the aspect ratio differs, the `object_pose` workflow node refuses to deproject (`aspect_mismatch`). Mimic joints are covered in [Mimic joints](/feature-reference/digital-twins/mimic-joints). Other schema helpers are in [Universal schema helpers](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset#universal-schema-helpers-python-sdk).

**Done when:** the twin shows the panels you expect (joint sliders, gripper, sensor windows) and nothing else.

## Make it simulate

1. Set world physics on the asset, for example `cw.assets.set_physics(asset.uuid, timestep=0.002, ...)`. All fields, defaults and tuning advice are in [Set physics properties](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset#set-physics-properties).
2. Enable MuJoCo on the asset under **Simulator compatibility**. New twins inherit it; existing twins keep their own setting. MuJoCo won't start if **any** twin in the environment isn't marked compatible.
3. In the environment editor, open **Simulation readiness → Prepare simulation**. It lists missing data and proposes fixes ([Simulator compatibility](/feature-reference/catalog/simulation-support)).
4. Run `cw.affect("simulation")`. This starts a MuJoCo instance, which is billable ([credits](/billing/credits)).

**Done when:** MuJoCo starts with your twin and it holds a commanded pose. **You are at L1.**

## Write the driver

The driver bridges your device's native protocol to the twin. Pick a starting point:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="ROS 2 driver">
    If your robot already has a ROS 2 driver, subclass `BaseROS2Driver` and forward topics with `from_ros`. You don't write MQTT code or message conversion:

    ```python
    from cyberwave.driver import CallbackGroup, DriverOperationMode, TopicSpec
    from cyberwave.driver.ros2 import BaseROS2Driver, Ros2TopicSpec, run_driver_main


    class MyArmDriver(BaseROS2Driver):
        REGISTRY_ID = "acme/my-arm"        # your asset's registry ID
        DEFAULT_NODE_NAME = "my_arm_driver"

        def define_interface(self, iface) -> None:
            iface.add_publisher(
                TopicSpec(
                    topic_slug="cyberwave/joint/{twin_uuid}/update",
                    payload_schema_ref="JointStatesPayload",
                    description="Forward /joint_states to the twin",
                ),
                CallbackGroup(),
                from_ros=Ros2TopicSpec(topic="/joint_states"),
                operation_modes=frozenset(DriverOperationMode),
            )


    if __name__ == "__main__":
        run_driver_main(MyArmDriver, anchor=__file__)
    ```

    `CW_ROS2_AUTO_ACTIVATE=true` is recommended during development. The SDK stamps `source_type=edge` on everything `from_ros` publishes. Full reference: [BaseROS2Driver](/feature-reference/edge/drivers/ros2-base-driver).

    {/* TODO(docs): confirm whether REGISTRY_ID accepts the unified asset slug (registry_id is deprecated in favour of slug). */}
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Python SDK">
    No ROS? Subclass [`BaseDriver`](/feature-reference/edge/drivers/base-driver-class) and call your vendor SDK. For arms and grippers, compose the [driver mixins](/feature-reference/edge/drivers/driver-mixins) rather than writing joint-command plumbing yourself.

    The official SDKs handle twin synchronization, file I/O and reconnection logic, so you focus on the hardware:

    - **Python SDK**: [`cyberwave`](https://pypi.org/project/cyberwave/)
    - **C++ SDK**: see the [C++ SDK docs](/overview/tools/cpp-sdk)

    ```bash
    pip install cyberwave
    ```

    A driver does two things: it **subscribes** to commands and turns them into hardware actions, and it **publishes** telemetry and state back up to the twin:

    ```python
    from cyberwave import Cyberwave
    import os, time

    cw = Cyberwave(api_key=os.environ["CYBERWAVE_API_KEY"], source_type="edge")
    twin_uuid = os.environ["CYBERWAVE_TWIN_UUID"]

    # --- Publish observed joint states up to the twin ---
    def on_hardware_state(names, positions):
        cw.data.publish("joint_states", {
            "ts": time.time(),
            "names": names,
            "positions": positions,
        })

    # --- Subscribe to incoming commands and drive the hardware ---
    def handle_command(cmd):
        if cmd["command"] == "stop":
            my_device.stop()
        elif cmd["command"] == "move_forward":
            my_device.move(cmd["data"].get("linear_x", 0.0))

    # Wire handle_command to cyberwave/twin/{twin_uuid}/command, then run your loop:
    while True:
        names, positions = my_device.read_joints()   # your native protocol here
        on_hardware_state(names, positions)
        time.sleep(0.05)
    ```

    `cw.data.publish(...)` writes to the **edge data bus**, which makes the data available to local worker containers and ML models with zero network overhead. Canonical channels include `joint_states`, `frames/default`, `position`, `imu` and `battery`. You can also publish over MQTT directly for cloud consumers (frontend, telemetry, workflows). Topics and payloads: [MQTT reference](/api-reference/mqtt/main).
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="AI driver skill">
    The **Cyberwave Driver skill** for [Claude Code](https://claude.ai/claude-code)
    asks a few questions about your hardware and scaffolds a driver project: the
    Dockerfile, local dev setup, `cw-driver.yml`, and a working twin connection.

## Declare the interface

Your driver's interface is a **`cw-driver.yml`** at the driver repo root, next to the `Dockerfile`. For ROS 2 drivers it's the exported `manifest.yaml` instead. It's the contract between your hardware bridge, the platform APIs, the web UI and the SDK. It lists which MQTT topics you use and which command strings you accept, so teleop overlays, SDK methods and agents line up with what the hardware actually does. Which file does what: [Driver config files](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset#driver-config-files).

Point `registry_ids` at your asset, then declare only the topics and commands your code handles:

```yaml
registry_ids:
  - acme-corp/acme-arm-v1   # the registry_id of YOUR asset
mqtt:
  schema_version: 1
  driver_family: python      # ros_cpp, python, android, ...
  joint:
    update:
      direction: both
      payload_schema_ref: JointStatesPayload
      description: Joint targets in, observed joint states out
  twin:
    command:
      direction: in
      payload_schema_ref: TwinCommandPayload
      description: Discrete and continuous commands for the arm
    telemetry:
      direction: out
      payload_schema_ref: TelemetryPayload
      description: Lifecycle and status JSON
  commands:
    supported:
      - stop                                 # discrete (one-shot)
      - { name: move_forward, continuous: true, rate_hz: 10 }  # stick-held
```

Each YAML path compiles to a flat MQTT topic. For example `mqtt.joint.update` becomes `cyberwave/joint/{twin_uuid}/update`, and `mqtt.twin.command` becomes `cyberwave/twin/{twin_uuid}/command`.

<Note>
  Only declare what you actually implement. Arm / joint-bus drivers should omit
  the WebRTC topics; camera video flows through child camera twins and the media
  service, not WebRTC topics on the arm twin.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Use the reference manifests as templates: the
  [SO-101 arm](https://github.com/cyberwave-os/cyberwave-edge-so101/blob/main/cw-driver.yml)
  for a joint bus plus command topic, or the
  [UGV Beast](https://github.com/cyberwave-os/cyberwave-edge-ros-ugv/blob/main/cw-driver.yml)
  for continuous locomotion. Copy the namespaces that match your robot and delete the rest.
</Tip>

For ROS 2 drivers, export the combined manifest at build time with `python main.py write-manifest`.

### Register it on your twin

By default the driver registers its own interface when it starts (`auto_register_interface = True` calls `twin.driver.set_schema(...)`). To register it without running the driver, or from CI:

```python
from cyberwave import Cyberwave

cw = Cyberwave(api_key="...")
twin = cw.twin(twin_id="acme/twins/my-arm")

# Compile the manifest, persist it on this twin, re-bind catalog methods
twin.driver.set_schema("./cw-driver.yml")   # or MyArmDriver.get_manifest()
print(twin.driver.get_supported_commands())

# Inspect the catalog the UI and SDK now use
print(twin.commands.get_schema()["commands"]["supported"])

# Call any command you declared
twin.commands.stop()
twin.commands.move_forward(linear_x=0.3, duration=0.5, rate_hz=10)
```

`set_schema` compiles the file on the platform (`POST /api/v1/twins/{uuid}/driver-schema`), writes `metadata.mqtt` on the twin, refreshes the catalog cache, and re-binds `twin.commands.<name>`. Re-run it whenever you change the manifest so teleop, agents and SDK callers stay aligned with your driver. Details: [Writing compatible drivers](/feature-reference/edge/drivers/writing-compatible-drivers).

<Info>
  `set_schema` updates **this twin's** metadata, so you can iterate without
  changing a shared asset definition. When the driver is solid, you can apply the
  same `metadata.mqtt` to the asset so every new twin inherits it.
</Info>

    Push to Docker Hub or to the Cyberwave registry `registry.cyberwave.com` (Enterprise; authenticate with `docker login registry.cyberwave.com` using your API key, see [Docker registry](/feature-reference/docker-registry)). Version tags are immutable there.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Bind the image to the asset">
    Edge Core reads the asset's `metadata.drivers` block and starts one container per linked twin:

    ```json
    {
      "drivers": {
        "default": {
          "docker_image": "registry.cyberwave.com/acme/my-arm-driver:v0.1.0",
          "params": ["--network", "host"]
        },
        "linux-aarch64-jetson": {
          "docker_image": "registry.cyberwave.com/acme/my-arm-driver:jetson-v0.1.0",
          "prefer_gpu": true
        }
      }
    }
    ```

    Edge Core picks the most specific platform key for the host and falls back to `default`. Key rules, multi-container `services` and env vars: [metadata.drivers keys](/feature-reference/catalog/upload-asset#metadatadrivers-keys).

    Set it on the asset from the SDK (read, merge, write), or edit a twin's metadata in the environment editor under **Advanced editing**. Changing the asset affects twins created afterwards.

    ```python
    asset = cw.assets.get("acme/catalog/my-arm")
    metadata = dict(asset.metadata or {})
    metadata["drivers"] = {"default": {"docker_image": "registry.cyberwave.com/acme/my-arm-driver:v0.1.0"}}
    cw.assets.update(asset.uuid, {"metadata": metadata})
    ```
  </Step>

    On the machine wired to your hardware, install the CLI and pair:

    <EdgeSetup />

    Run `sudo cyberwave pair` and select your twin. Edge Core pulls and runs your driver image with the environment variables injected automatically, beside the device, on the robot, in the cloud, or on your laptop. Watch it with `cyberwave edge status` and `cyberwave edge logs -f`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Done when:** on a clean device, `sudo cyberwave pair` followed by selecting your twin pulls the image, the container starts, and the twin goes online. More on Edge Core: [Cyberwave Edge](/feature-reference/edge/overview).

## Make it fail safely

Your driver must fail loudly and stop safely.

- **Exit non-zero** when required hardware is missing (a missing `/dev/video*` device, a disconnected peripheral). Edge Core then raises `driver_start_failure` and applies its restart policy. `run_driver_main` exits with code 1 when the driver raises.
- **Stop on silence.** If commands stop arriving mid-teleop, bring the robot to a safe state. Zenoh command channels support `watchdog_ms` with an `on_command_timeout` hook. For MQTT, implement the timeout in your driver.
- **Raise alerts** operators can see: `create_twin_alert(...)` or `AlertManager` from the SDK ([Alerts](/feature-reference/edge/drivers/alerts)).
- **Calibration:** a twin with `can_actuate` shows a **Re-calibrate driver** action in Live mode. Test it against your robot, and document any vendor calibration steps on the asset.

## Record data and train a policy

1. Dock your cameras to the robot as child twins with an [asset kit](/feature-reference/catalog/asset-kits). Edge Core passes child camera twins to the parent driver in `CYBERWAVE_CHILD_TWIN_UUIDS`.
2. Record teleop episodes and export them (LeRobot is one of the export formats).
3. Fine-tune a VLA and assign it with `twin.policy.assign(...)`.

Worked example: [SO-101 teleop dataset](/tutorials/so101-teleop-dataset) → [Train a VLA](/tutorials/train-vla-cyberwave).

**Done when:** a policy trained on your robot's data drives the robot. **You are at L4.**

## Publish your robot

Give the asset (and kit) a clear description, bill of materials and troubleshooting notes. Then switch the asset's visibility to **Public**. Public assets are visible to anyone on the platform.

To be listed with a support level, or for closed-source driver distribution, [contact Cyberwave](https://cyberwave.com/contact-us) or email [info@cyberwave.com](mailto:info@cyberwave.com) with your target level and the pass-test results.
